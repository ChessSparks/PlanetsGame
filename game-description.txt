PLANETS GAME
"A crash. A signal. A choice."

============================================================
OVERVIEW
============================================================
Planets Game is a third-person, story-driven 3D exploration game built
in the browser with Three.js. You play a stranded pilot whose ship
crash-lands on a remote world. Guided by Corthana, your ship's onboard
AI, you repair your ship, escape the planet, and follow a trail of
clues across a moon and a client's homeworld — only to discover the
true purpose of the cargo you've been hired to deliver, and to make a
final choice about whether to go through with it.

The game is played entirely on the surface of small spherical planets
(walking all the way around them, gravity always pointing "down"
toward the core), interspersed with an on-rails rocket-ascent flying
sequence, cinematic ship travel/cutscenes between worlds, and four
distinct hand-built logic puzzles that gate story progress.

============================================================
STORY
============================================================
Your ship crashes on a quiet, empty planet under a trinary sun. There's
no sign of life anywhere — no animals, no people, nothing except
untouched plant life. Corthana, still online after the crash, helps you
repair the ship, and together you discover that "quiet" was not always
this planet's natural state.

Once repaired and refueled, you launch, reach orbit, and set a course
for the Moon — a waypoint where an ancient console reveals the location
of valuable crystal deposits. Corthana is uneasy about what they might
be for.

From there you travel to the Client's Homeworld: a hostile, industrial
world dominated by a single spire, guarded by patrol sentries. You
track down a map beacon, cross the planet, and finally reach the spire
— where the client reveals, without hesitation or shame, exactly what
the crystals power: a weapon capable of silently wiping out every
non-plant life-sign in a system, from orbit, with no warning. It's the
same fate that already befell the first planet you crash-landed on.

The game then puts the choice in your hands:
  - Hand over the crystals — get paid, escape cleanly, and live with
    what you enabled.
  - Refuse — fight your way to the ship past newly-hostile sentries,
    escape empty-handed, and live with yourself instead.

Either choice leads to its own ending narrated by Corthana.

============================================================
STRUCTURE / GAME FLOW
============================================================
1. GROUND (Home Planet) — crash-landing, repair & fuel puzzle
2. ASCENT — rocket launch minigame, climbing to orbit
3. TRAVEL CUTSCENE — cinematic transition to the Moon
4. MOON — exploration, star chart puzzle, crystal collection
5. TRAVEL CUTSCENE — cinematic transition to the Client's Homeworld
6. CLIENT WORLD — the game's largest level: trek to the spire, the
   reveal, the moral choice, and the escape
7. TRAVEL CUTSCENE (return) — leads back to a fresh Ground phase

Progress autosaves at each phase, so players can leave and resume
(a "Continue" option appears on the title screen) instead of always
replaying from the crash.

============================================================
PHASE-BY-PHASE GAMEPLAY
============================================================

--- GROUND (Home Planet) ---
An in-engine crash cutscene opens the game: your ship tumbles in from
altitude on a banking arc and smashes into the surface. Once you regain
control, you explore a small spherical planet (walk fully around it —
gravity always points to the core) filled with forests, meadows,
houses, a volcano, wildlife, drifting clouds, and a distant view of
your home planet in the sky.

Objectives:
 - Find the "key finder" device.
 - Use it to locate 3 hidden ship parts scattered across the planet.
   Two are guarded by patrol drones that give chase (and kill on
   contact) if you wander too close — outrunning them is always
   possible, but getting caught resets your current run.
 - Return to the ship and repair it.
 - Find a nearby smaller volcano and solve a sliding-puzzle minigame
   to secure a fuel cell.
 - Interact with the repaired, fueled ship to launch.

Controls: W/↑ walk, S/↓ back up, A/D turn, Shift run, Space jump,
E interact.

--- ASCENT (Rocket Launch) ---
A vertical side-scrolling flight sequence. After a scripted liftoff,
you pilot the rocket up through the atmosphere and into orbit,
thrusting to fight gravity, strafing left/right to dodge birds and
patrol drones, and collecting floating fuel cells along the way.
Fuel drains while thrusting and refills from pickups; getting hit costs
fuel and grants brief invulnerability. Difficulty (obstacle speed and
density) ramps continuously all the way to orbit rather than easing off
near the top. Reaching orbit altitude wins the level; running out of
fuel before then ends it in failure and restarts the climb.

--- TRAVEL CUTSCENE ---
A short cinematic space-flight sequence bridges each planetary phase,
with its own color palette and music theme per leg of the journey
(Home Orbit → Moon → Client's Homeworld → New Assignment).

--- MOON ---
A lower-gravity spherical world (floatier jumps, "moon bounce" physics)
scattered with rocks. After an in-engine landing cutscene (a controlled,
braking descent — the opposite feel from the home planet's violent
crash), you explore to find an ancient beacon/console. Solving its star
chart puzzle reveals the locations of 5 hidden crystal deposits, which
only then appear in the world to be collected. Once all crystals are
gathered, returning to the ship departs for the Client's Homeworld.
The home planet is visible, huge, in the moon's sky — a visual payoff
for the ground-to-orbit arc.

--- CLIENT WORLD ---
The biggest level in the game. A hostile, industrial planet: a long
walk from the landing site, through generated building districts and
scattered ruins, to a distant spire — all while patrol sentries circle
overhead (initially non-hostile).

Objectives:
 - Find a map beacon and solve a circuit puzzle to reveal the spire's
   location.
 - Trek to the spire and interact with it, triggering the story's
   central reveal and dialogue sequence.
 - Make the choice: hand over the crystals, or refuse.
 - Either choice sets the sentries hostile and hunting.
 - Find the control tower and solve a cipher puzzle to disable the
   sentries.
 - Return to the ship to trigger the ending sequence matching your
   choice.

============================================================
PUZZLES (MINIGAMES)
============================================================
Four distinct hand-built puzzle types gate major story beats:
 1. Sliding Puzzle — secures the fuel cell on the home planet.
 2. Star Chart Puzzle — reveals the moon's crystal deposit locations.
 3. Circuit Puzzle — reveals the client world's spire location.
 4. Cipher Puzzle — disables the hostile sentries at the control tower.

============================================================
CHARACTERS
============================================================
 - The Player — a stranded pilot/courier, silent protagonist.
 - Corthana — your ship's onboard AI, a constant companion voice
   throughout: helps diagnose the crash, reacts to discoveries, grows
   suspicious of the crystal cargo, and ultimately reflects on whatever
   choice you make at the end.
 - The Client — heard only as a voice from within the spire; reveals
   the true purpose of the crystals without remorse.

============================================================
TECH / PRESENTATION NOTES
============================================================
 - Built with Three.js (WebGL) and Vite.
 - Every planet is a walkable sphere with its own gravity, terrain,
   decorations, and hazards.
 - Minimap, HUD (fuel/altitude bars, collectible counters), dynamic
   objective banners, toast notifications, and text-to-speech narration
   for Corthana's lines.
 - Dev menu (hamburger icon) allows jumping directly to any phase for
   testing.
 - Settings menu accessible via Escape/gear icon, pausing gameplay.
 - Each scene/level is code-split and lazy-loaded so players never
   download content for levels they haven't reached yet.
